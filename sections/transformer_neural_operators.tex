\subsection{Transolver as an Integral Operator}
Transolver~\citep{wu2024transolver} approximates continuous integral operators through Physics-Attention. Let $\Omega$ denote the physical domain, and let $\rho$ denote the sampling measure induced by the chosen grid or mesh discretization policy. A discretized example consists of pointwise samples
$\{(x_i,a_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}\mapsto\{u_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $x_i\sim\rho$, $a_i=a(x_i)$ is the input field, and $u_i=u(x_i)$ is the output field. A neural operator learns an operator $\mathcal{G}:a\mapsto u$ with a parameterized network $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$.

Physics-Attention projects point features into $M$ slice weights, applies attention to the resulting latent physical states, and broadcasts the updated states back to the points. Let $h_i\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ denote the $C$-dimensional feature embedding of $(x_i,a_i)$. The slice weights are
\begin{equation}
    w_i
    = \operatorname{Softmax}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Linear}(h_i)}{\tau_0}\right)
    \in\mathbb{R}^{M},
    \qquad
    \sum_{j=1}^{M}w_{i,j}=1,
    \label{eq:transolver_slice_weights}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_0$ is the temperature. The corresponding physics token is a normalized weighted average
\begin{equation}
    u_{s,j}^{(N)}
    = \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i,j}h_i}
           {\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i,j}}
    \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}
    u_{s,j}^{\rho}
    = \frac{\int_{\Omega}w_j(x)h(x)\,\mathrm{d}\rho(x)}
           {\int_{\Omega}w_j(x)\,\mathrm{d}\rho(x)}.
    \label{eq:transolver_physics_state}
\end{equation}

Writing $u_s^{(N)}=[u_{s,1}^{(N)},\ldots,u_{s,M}^{(N)}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times C}$ and $q,k,v=\operatorname{Linear}(u_s^{(N)})$, the discrete state attention and its continuous integral counterpart can be written together as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    u_{s,j}'
    &= \sum_{t=1}^{M}
      \frac{\exp\!\left(q_j^{\top}k_t/\tau\right)}
           {\sum_{p=1}^{M}\exp\!\left(q_j^{\top}k_p/\tau\right)}v_t \\
    &\xrightarrow[M\to\infty]{}
    \mathcal{A}[u_s^{\rho}](\xi_s') \\
    &:= \int_{\Omega_s}
      \frac{\exp\!\left(q(u_s^{\rho}(\xi_s'))^{\top}k(u_s^{\rho}(\xi_s))/\tau\right)}
           {\int_{\Omega_s}\exp\!\left(q(u_s^{\rho}(\xi_s'))^{\top}k(u_s^{\rho}(\eta_s))/\tau\right)\,\mathrm{d}\eta_s}
      v(u_s^{\rho}(\xi_s))\,\mathrm{d}\xi_s.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:transolver_state_attention}
\end{equation}
where $u_s^{\rho}(\xi_s)$ is the continuous physics-token field on the slice domain $\Omega_s$, $\xi_s'$ is a query location, and $\tau$ is the attention temperature. Finally, the updated tokens are mapped back to the point space through the slice weights,
\begin{equation}
    h_i' = \sum_{j=1}^{M}w_{i,j}u_{s,j}',
    \qquad i=1,\ldots,N.
    \label{eq:transolver_deslice}
\end{equation}
Thus, Physics-Attention replaces the $N\times N$ point interaction with operations of complexity $\mathcal{O}(NMC+M^2C)$ for $M\ll N$. During training, the point sets used by $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ are typically generated according to the prescribed measure $\rho$.
