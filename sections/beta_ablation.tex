\subsection{Ablation of Measure-Aware Physics-Attention}
\label{app:beta_ablation}

We vary $\beta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ in the compensation weights
$c_i^\beta$ for PI, CPG, and CA. Operator and head checkpoints, coarse and
final point sets, and reconstruction stay fixed within each policy. We use
the original test populations, retained fractions of $37.5\%$, $50\%$, and
$75\%$, and sampling seeds 0, 1, and 2. Setting $\beta=0$ recovers the
original point-to-slice aggregation, while $\beta=1$ applies full compensation.

Let $A_{m,s}(\beta)$ denote the trapezoidal error--budget area for policy
$m$ and seed $s$. The ordinate in Figure~\ref{fig:beta_ablation} is
\begin{equation}
    R_m(\beta)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{s=0}^{2}
    \frac{A_{m,s}(\beta)}{A_{m,s}(0)}.
    \label{eq:beta_relative_area}
\end{equation}
Normalization precedes averaging, with $R_m(0)=1$ for every policy.
Table~\ref{tab:beta_ablation} instead uses the common Uniform-normalized
rAUEC to compare $\beta=0$ with the main-experiment exponent $\beta^{\star}$.
The table isolates removal of correction, while the curves show sensitivity
to its strength.

For CPG, full correction reduces the error--budget area by $89.7\%$ on
Airfoil field prediction and $93.9\%$ on Pipe. Darcy CPG and CA improve from
rAUECs of 4.278 and 4.265 to 0.642 and 0.640, becoming better than Uniform
with the sampled locations unchanged. On ShapeNet-Car drag prediction,
$\beta^{\star}=0.75$ reduces the area by $20.2\%$ for CPG and $20.3\%$ for CA.

On SuperWing field prediction, CPG and CA gain $66.1\%$ and $69.1\%$ from
full correction; their DrivAerML drag gains are $63.3\%$ and $67.3\%$.
For both datasets, $\beta=1$ gives the lowest area among the tested exponents
for every policy and metric.

The preferred strength varies across tasks. ShapeNet-Car CPG and CA attain
their lowest areas at $\beta=0.75$, while PI favors $\beta=0.5$. Increasing
PI to $\beta=1$ raises its drag area by $5.2\%$ relative to no correction.
Elasticity gains only approximately $0.5$--$3.1\%$ at the main-experiment
settings.

\paragraph{Effect on physics tokens.}
For fixed layer inputs $h_i$ and slice assignments $w_{i,j}$, define
\begin{equation}
    p_{i,j}(\beta)
    =\frac{w_{i,j}c_i^\beta}{\sum_k w_{k,j}c_k^\beta},
    \qquad
    z_j(\beta)=\sum_i p_{i,j}(\beta)h_i.
\end{equation}
The direct derivative is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial z_j}{\partial\beta}
    =\operatorname{Cov}_{p_j(\beta)}(h,\log c).
    \label{eq:beta_token_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
The identity holds for the displayed normalization with fixed inputs;
implementation adds a small denominator stabilizer. Later layers also
receive propagated changes from earlier layers. Direct sensitivity depends
on feature--log-weight covariance, vanishing when features or compensation
weights are constant within a slice.

Exact density correction recovers reference-measure aggregation with
sufficient sampling (Section~\ref{sec:density_consistent_transolver}). At
finite budgets, matching cell mass does not recover within-cell feature
variation: a Voronoi weight can count reference nodes exactly while its
retained feature only approximates their aggregate. For reconstructed
prediction $F_\beta$ and differentiable task loss $\mathcal L$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm d\mathcal L}{\mathrm d\beta}
    =\left\langle\nabla_F\mathcal L,
      \frac{\mathrm dF_\beta}{\mathrm d\beta}\right\rangle.
    \label{eq:beta_task_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
The sign of this inner product determines whether increasing $\beta$ locally
improves the target metric. Reference-measure consistency therefore differs
from minimizing error against physical ground truth.

\paragraph{Sensitivity of aerodynamic ratios.}
For Airfoil geometry 1059, seed 1, and budget $37.5\%$, PI at $\beta=0.5$
has pressure error $0.865\%$ but predicts $C_L=2.48\times10^{-5}$ instead
of $0.024676$. Its absolute ratio error, 1438.20, contributes $90.0\%$ of
the PI error--budget area at this exponent and seed across the 110 test
geometries. Away from $C_L=0$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm d(C_D/C_L)}{\mathrm d\beta}
    =\frac{C_L\,\mathrm dC_D/\mathrm d\beta
           -C_D\,\mathrm dC_L/\mathrm d\beta}{C_L^2}.
    \label{eq:beta_ratio_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
The near-zero denominator explains the peak. On this geometry, increasing
$\beta$ from $0.75$ to $1$ improves pressure error from $0.703\%$ to
$0.617\%$ but worsens ratio error from 0.677 to 1.079. A field norm measures
error magnitude, whereas forces depend on signed surface integrals and the
ratio adds lift-denominator sensitivity. These predictions show why field
and aerodynamic metrics can prefer different correction strengths.
